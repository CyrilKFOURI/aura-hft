# Honest backtest of the wired OBI strategy

**Short conclusion: on the real 2019-01-30 data, the strategy as the RTL implements it loses money after costs, in every configuration tested, both pre-market and in the regular session.** The OBI signal carries a small amount of directional information (positive mid-to-mid upper bound in the regular session), but it is smaller than what adverse selection on the passive entry and the spread paid on exit cost. A single half-day proves nothing, in either direction.

Reproduce (≈ 35 min of download, ≈ 15 min of extraction, 1 min for the grid):

```bash
python data/fetch.py --messages 60000000
python -m research.obi_backtest
python -m pytest research
```

Full raw results, generated by the script: [`obi_backtest/results/results.md`](obi_backtest/results/results.md) (and `results.json`).

## 1. Data

- NASDAQ TotalView-ITCH 5.0, public sample of **2019-01-30**, first 60 M messages (stream read continuously, SHA-256 `a714f8cd…f74d`). Coverage: 04:00:00 → **10:11:06 ET**. That is 5 h 30 of pre-market and only **41 minutes** of regular session.
- Symbols: AAPL, MSFT, TSLA, QQQ. Books rebuilt by `sim/golden_model/golden_order_book.py`, validated at 0 divergence against MeatPy on this file. Book events: AAPL 396,064, MSFT 253,055, TSLA 179,979, QQQ 701,286.

## 2. Trading rule replicated (read from the RTL)

| Element | RTL | Backtest |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Signal | `obi_signal.sv`: buy if `(Vb−Va)·2^31 ≥ T_buy·(Vb+Va)`, sell if `≤ T_sell·(Vb+Va)`, Q1.31, size at the best level only | Same exact integer comparison (`model.obi_signals`), `T_sell = −T_buy` |
| Order | `risk_ouch_top.sv`: buy at the **best bid**, sell at the **best ask**, `cfg_default_order_shares` | 100 shares, price = top of book at decision time |
| Risk | `pre_trade_risk.sv`: kill switch, max shares, price collar, per-order notional, cumulative notional | Same rules and same priority (`model.risk_check`); testbench config: 1000 shares, collar $0.50, $50k per order; **cumulative cap disabled** for the research (see section 5) |

**What the RTL does not do and the backtest had to add** (accepted differences, needed to have a strategy that can be evaluated):

1. **One position at a time.** The RTL emits an order on every BBO update as long as the signal holds (limited only by `tx_busy`, ~77 ns). It has no position management, no cancel, no exit.
2. **Entry order lifetime = horizon H**, then cancel (cancel latency ignored).
3. **Exit**: aggressive (taker) order at the opposite touch H after the fill, plus the latency.
4. Filter: both sides of the book non-empty, not crossed, time ≥ 04:00 (the RTL would also fire on a one-sided book, OBI = ±1).

## 3. Fill model (explicit assumptions)

The virtual order arrives at `t_decision + latency` and joins **the back of the queue** at its price. Each book event carries the entry time of the real order it hit (= its priority).

- **Marketable on arrival** (the market moved during the latency, ask ≤ our buy price): immediate fill at the opposite touch, **taker**.
- **Price-time priority**: executions (`E`/`C`) at the same price against orders **older** than ours do not fill us. An execution at the same price against an order **younger** than ours would have hit us first (FIFO): it fills us share for share. This is the queue position at entry, tracked exactly.
- **Cancels** (`X`/`D`/`U`) of orders ahead of us: move us forward, never fill us.
- **Trade-through**: an execution on our side at a price worse than ours, or the opposite touch crossing our price → fill of the remainder.
- Partial fills are possible; the remainder is cancelled at expiry.
- Not modelled: hidden/non-displayed orders, impact of our own orders on the market (100 shares), depth at exit (100 shares are assumed available at the touch), exchange-side queueing for OUCH.

No look-ahead: every decision reads the book state at the current event; every price at a time t reads the last row with timestamp ≤ t (`model.row_at`). A test checks this (`test_strategy_end_to_end_no_lookahead`).

Latency between 0 and 100 µs changes almost nothing: the strategy is **passive** (it joins the back of the queue) and holds for seconds. An FPGA latency advantage is of no use for this rule; it would matter for a strategy that takes liquidity or that wins queue priority.

### Naive mid-to-mid upper bound (clearly unrealistic)

Entry and exit at the mid, no costs, no queue, no latency. Regular session: +0.004 to +0.012 $/share at 1–10 s, 95 % CIs positive (e.g. threshold 0.4, 10 s: +0.0124 $/share, 953 signals, CI [+296 ; +1,659] $ for 100 shares). Pre-market: CIs that contain 0. **The signal therefore has a little directional information, on the order of 1 tick over 10 s, but the half-spread paid on exit and the adverse selection on the passive entry (we are filled mostly when the price moves against us: hit rate of 12–34 % against ~50 % for the bound) largely wipe it out.**

### Literal RTL behaviour

With threshold 0.4 and the testbench config ($500k cumulative cap), the RTL emits an order on every BBO update that satisfies the signal (AAPL: 199,392 orders over the period), and the cumulative cap, which never decreases, **blocks everything from 04:03 (AAPL, QQQ), 04:35 (MSFT), 05:12 (TSLA)** after 16 to 47 orders. As it stands, the pipeline is not a deployable strategy: position/cancel management is needed, in hardware or software, before talking about PnL.

## 6. Statistics

- PnL per trade = gross − fees of both legs. Hit rate = share of trades with net PnL > 0. Max drawdown on the cumulative net PnL curve.
- 95 % CI: moving-block bootstrap (5-minute blocks) on net PnL aggregated per minute, 2000 draws, fixed seed. Minutes without a trade count as 0.

## 7. Limitations

- **A single day**, and only 41 minutes of regular session, including the open (an atypical regime). No stability conclusion is possible; it would take dozens of days (a full day and other dates).
- A grid of 3 thresholds × 3 horizons × 4 latencies explored on the same data: no configuration is a winner, so there is no risk of selecting a false positive, but a "less losing" configuration (0.4 / 60 s) is not an edge.
- Fees/rebates: tier assumptions; a higher rebate (0.0030) would improve the net by only 0.001 $/share, far from the measured losses.
- Hidden orders, depth at exit, market impact and cancel latency are not modelled; trade-through fills are probably optimistic for a virtual order.
- Taker exit only; a passive exit or market-making logic (Avellaneda-Stoikov, experimental) is not tested here.
- No check of LULD/halts, nor of the maximum net position.
